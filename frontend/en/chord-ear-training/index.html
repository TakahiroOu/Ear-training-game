Chord ear training

Three notes played together can sound bright, dark, tense or floating, depending on the type of chord. Linking each sound to a word helps you recognize chords quickly in dictation, accompaniment and songwriting.

Try it: tell the triads apart

The four triads

TriadBuilt from the rootCharacter
Majormajor 3rd + perfect 5th (C–E–G)bright and stable
Minorminor 3rd + perfect 5th (C–E♭–G)darker and softer
Diminishedminor 3rd + diminished 5th (B–D–F)narrow and tense, wants to move on
Augmentedmajor 3rd + augmented 5th (C–E–G♯)floating and mysterious

How to tell them apart

  1. Major or minor first. Only the middle note (the 3rd) differs: bright or dark.
  2. Then: stable or not? Major and minor sound settled; diminished and augmented don't.
  3. Diminished vs. augmented. Diminished feels squeezed inward, augmented stretched outward.
  4. Sing the notes. Arpeggiate the chord ("do–mi–sol") to hear where the 3rd and 5th are.
