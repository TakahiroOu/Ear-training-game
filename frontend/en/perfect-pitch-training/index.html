Perfect pitch training

Perfect pitch (also called absolute pitch) is the ability to name a note just by hearing it, without comparing it to any other note: you hear a sound and know "that's an A". Try it below: one note plays with no reference, and you name it.

Can adults learn perfect pitch?

Full perfect pitch is thought to depend a lot on musical experience in early childhood, and it is generally considered hard for adults to develop it in the same way. Still, regular note-naming practice can help you remember notes you hear often (such as C or A) and name them correctly more often.

For most musicians, what matters day to day is relative pitch, and it can be trained at any age. Practice it alongside note naming with interval ear training and chord ear training.

How to practice

  1. Start with the 7 white keys. Like the quiz above, begin with C to B only, then add the black keys.
  2. Pick one anchor note. For example, listen to A every day until you remember it, and think of other notes as steps from it.
  3. Sing before you answer. Singing the note you heard helps you remember its pitch.
  4. A little every day. Five minutes a day works better than one long session a week.
